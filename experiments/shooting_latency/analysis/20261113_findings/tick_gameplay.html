Tanks tick 24 times a second. Jets tick 48.

Our recordings found a fixed 24 Hz tick for ground vehicles (the same at 60 and 120 FPS) and a 48 Hz network rate in air battles. Below is what one step means for a jet at 2000 km/h and a tank at 80 km/h, crossing your view or climbing and diving.

11.6 ma jet at 2000 km/h travels per 48 Hz step, about ¾ of its own length
0.93 ma tank at 80 km/h travels per 24 Hz tick, about 1/8 of its length

When the target changes course

The other player's actions reach your screen about 160 ms (tanks) to 170 ms (jets) later than they reach theirs (measured: their gunshot heard on your PC minus their own). How that shows up depends on how the game draws them.

Drawing the past

If the game drew positions the same way it plays gunshots, a 2000 km/h jet would always appear 94 m behind where it really is, and a tank 3.6 m behind. The path would be correct, just late.

Guessing the present

Gaijin says War Thunder extrapolates: it predicts where the target is now. In straight flight that's spot on. When the target turns or brakes, the guess is wrong until the news arrives, then it corrects.

Manoeuvreafter one step (jet 21 ms, tank 42 ms)after 100 msafter the remote delay (jet 170 ms, tank 160 ms)

Error of a straight-line guess = ½ · acceleration · time². The time is how long ago the newest information about the target was true.

What this means in a fight

  • In a tank, your trigger has a 0–42 ms random delay (measured). At 80 km/h that's under a metre of target travel and barely matters. In planes we saw no such wait in the gunshot sound; if bullets still start on Anton's 48 Hz grid, that's 0–11.6 m on a 2000 km/h crossing jet.
  • Direction doesn't change the distance. Left, right, climbing or diving, the jet covers 11.6 m per 48 Hz step. What matters is how much of that motion is across your line of sight: a head-on or tail-chase target barely moves on screen.
  • Range turns metres into angle. At 500 m one jet step is 23 mils (1.3°); at 2000 m it's 6 mils. Close-range deflection shots on fast jets suffer most.
  • Hard turns show up late. A 9 g break starts on your screen roughly 170 ms after it began. A straight-line guess is about 1.3 m off by then, and the corrected position catches up over the following frames.
  • Tanks are barely affected by the tick itself. At 80 km/h one tick is 0.93 m. The ~150 ms between your click and your own gun firing (3.3 m) matters more than the tick does.

What's measured and what's estimated

  • measured The 24 Hz ground tick: your shot waits for the next tick. Seen on both PCs at 120 and 60 FPS; every blind frequency scan picked 24.000 Hz.
  • measured Click → own gunshot ≈ 150 ms (148 ms at 120 FPS, 153 ms at 60 FPS). Click → heard on the other PC 305–332 ms, median used here 315 ms.
  • measured Server sends updates about 24.3 times a second in ground battles (two servers) and on a ≈ 49 Hz grid in air battles (one server, one run).
  • measured In the air run, about 1 in 4 short taps never played on the other PC (49 % heard for taps under 50 ms).
  • estimated The split of the extra ~160 ms into ping (60–70 ms) and a buffer of about 2 ticks. This comes from medians plus the in-game ping, not a direct measurement.
  • estimated How positions are drawn. We measured gunshot sounds, not positions. Gaijin's own statement is that positions are extrapolated.
  • assumed Body lengths: jet 16 m, tank 7 m. Manoeuvres: 9 g and 5 g turns, a 0.6 g hard brake.
